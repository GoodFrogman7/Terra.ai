# Google Pixel — Quickstart KB (Sample)

# 1) Enable Call Screen (Pixel Call Assist)
Call Screen helps you screen calls and decline spam automatically.

**Steps**
1. Open the **Phone** app.  
2. Tap **More** → **Settings** → **Spam and Call Screen**/**Call Screen**.  
3. Choose your **Protection level**. On Pixel 7 and above, you can auto‑screen spam, suspected fakes, and first‑time callers.  
4. Follow on‑screen instructions if prompted.

**Official docs**  
- Screen your calls before you answer them — Phone app Help: https://support.google.com/phoneapp/answer/9118387  
- Call Assist overview (store article): https://store.google.com/intl/en/ideas/articles/pixel-call-assist-phone-screen/

## 2) Dual SIM, eSIM, and DSDS on Pixel
Pixels support Dual SIM Dual Standby (DSDS). Availability depends on model and carrier.

**Key points**
- Pixel 7/7 Pro and later can use **two eSIM profiles at once** if supported by your carrier.  
- You can also use **one physical SIM + one eSIM** (DSDS), depending on your model and carrier.  
- For specific Pixel 9/9a details and SKUs, check your carrier support pages.

**Official docs**  
- How to use dual SIMs on your Pixel phone (requirements & setup): https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/9449293  
- Connect to mobile networks / eSIM overview hub: https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/2926415  
- IMEI info for eSIM/DSDS (IMEI2): https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/10402530

## 3) Transfer your data to a new Pixel
Use the Pixel setup flow to copy apps, photos, messages, and settings.

**Steps (high‑level)**
1. Power on the new Pixel and follow the setup wizard.  
2. Choose **Express** to transfer your Google Account and backed‑up data, or **Customize** to pick specific items.  
3. Use the included Quick Switch Adapter or cable for fastest transfer, or restore from cloud backup.

**Official docs**  
- Transfer data from your previous device to a Pixel: https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/7129955  
- Set up your Pixel phone: https://support.google.com/pixelphone/answer/7158475  
- Guidebook: Transfer and restore from backup: https://guidebooks.google.com/pixel/transfer-your-data/how-to-switch-from-android

## 4) Find a lost Pixel (Find Hub / Find My Device)
Ensure device location and the Find network are enabled so you can locate, lock, or erase your phone if lost.

**Prepare**
1. On your Pixel, open **Settings** → **Google** → **All services** → **Personal & device safety** → **Find Hub** (naming may vary).  
2. Turn on **Allow device to be located** and join the Find network where available.

**Use it when lost**
- Go to **android.com/find** (Find Hub / Find My Device) to ring, locate, lock, or erase your device.  
- Some recent Pixels can be located even when offline or powered off for a short time (feature availability varies by model/region).

**Official docs**  
- Be ready to find a lost device (account help): https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/3265955  
- Find, secure, or erase a lost Android device: https://support.google.com/android/answer/6160491  
- Find Hub (about): https://www.google.com/android/find/about

**Background articles**  
- Android’s Find network upgrades (context): https://www.wired.com/story/android-find-my-device-upgrade-whats-new

## 5) Troubleshooting tips (quick)
- **Wi‑Fi drops**: Toggle Airplane mode, forget/reconnect network, reboot router/phone, check for system updates.  
- **Battery drain**: Review battery usage, disable high refresh rate/Always‑On display temporarily, check recently installed apps.  
- **eSIM activation fails**: Confirm carrier eSIM support for your model; ensure IMEI2 is correct and device is on latest software.
